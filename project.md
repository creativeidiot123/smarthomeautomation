# HomeDJ project facts

- House rules (Lights → Rules on both phones): a small text language (`when <light> turned off for 1m | turned on [for 5m] | off and on [within 1m] | on and off [within 1m] [at 00:00-07:00]`, `if <light> was|is on/off/N%/above N%/below N%/A%-B% and at … / not … / else / end` (`was` = record before the switch, `is` = when the rule starts), steps `all|<light>|<group> on/off/0–100%/+N%/-N%`, `all <value> except <lights/groups>`, top-level `<light> starts off|on|as before` (Samsung writes the bulb's own Matter StartUpOnOff, OnOff 0x4003, so a light can stay dark when its wall switch gives it power; kept by the bulb, not in HomeDJ storage), `scene <name> on/off`, `schedule <name> on/off`, `schedules on/off/now`, `music on/off`) saved on Samsung in SharedPreferences `homedj_house_rules` (`script`). New authenticated LAN commands `houseRules` and `houseRulesSave` (`script`); replies carry `script`. Samsung polls General Diagnostics RebootCount/UpTime of watched bulbs every 6 s via the existing connectedhomeip read path (no subscription); needs Remote control on. No new permission, port, dependency or WorkManager work.

- Lights setup from both phones: the authenticated LAN adds `lightsDiscover`, `lightsPair` (`setupCode`, optional `name` and integer `discriminator`) and `lightsForget` (`id`); replies add optional `lightsSetup: true`, and discovery adds at most 16 `discovered` name/discriminator entries. Samsung validates setup payloads and selected discriminators before the existing `Lights`/`MatterLights` owners run. Pairing has a 135-second server budget including owner-queue time and a 150-second Controller reply budget; discovery/forget use 15/40-second server budgets. No automatic mutation retry; lost confirmations refresh status. Older Samsungs keep setup unavailable with an update hint. No persisted keys, permissions, SDK versions, dependencies, framing or port change. Contract: [docs/controller-light-setup.md](docs/controller-light-setup.md).

- Lights experience: both phones have compact bulb/group controls, separate Lights / Schedule / Automation views, explicit one-bulb state/capability reads, up to 16 named fixed-member scenes, named weekly rules with pause/duplicate/run-now and a weekly agenda, sunrise/sunset offsets using locally calculated approximate solar times and manually entered coordinates, native bulb fades (0–3600 s), optional fresh authenticated Home conditions, templates and 12 recent schedule results. Samsung remains the sole owner. New optional `homedj_lights.experience` JSON holds scenes/location/history/zone; bulb `reading`, rule `name`/`enabled`/`time`/`offset`/`sceneId`/`activeAfter`/`onlyHome`, and change `fadeSeconds` are additive. New authenticated commands: `lightsSceneSave/Delete/Apply`, `lightsRuleSave/Delete/Enabled/Run`, `lightsLocation`, `lightsPresence`, `lightsRead` (the slash-separated suffixes denote separate command names); optional reply `lightsVersion=1` and snapshot `experience`. Old records/commands still read; old Controller saves preserve existing advanced fields, but advanced execution requires updated builds on both phones. No dependency, permission, service, WorkManager identity, port or pairing/fabric change. Contract and verification scope: [docs/lights-experience.md](docs/lights-experience.md).
- Scene editor "Set each light": per-light power, brightness and colour, seeded from what the editor shows (choose, capture or saved); stored in the existing per-bulb scene changes, so no persisted or wire change.
- Scene juggle: a scene variation subsetting (shown when the scene's lights have at least two colours) that swaps those lights' colours at random every 15 s / 30 s / 1 min / 5 min while variation keeps running; runs in Samsung's variation loop, swaps never persisted. Persisted additively: scene `j`, `experience.variation.juggle` and per-light `j`; optional reply `lightsJuggle: true`. Worst-case reply frame stays within the 2 KB spare. No new command, port, permission or dependency.
- Applying a scene (tap or schedule) on Samsung also turns off every paired light left out of it that is not recorded off, with the scene's fade, as an individual change like the scene's own lights. No persisted, wire or command change.
- Scene music: a scene can link up to 10 of Samsung's saved playlists (scene editor on both phones; the OnePlus picks from the Library list Samsung sent it). However the scene is applied (tap on either phone, light schedule, house-rule `scene <name> on`; not house rules' `schedules now`, which only puts lights back), Samsung then plays their cached songs shuffled (≤2,000), replacing what is playing; the same scene's unfinished queue only resumes; nothing starts while the OnePlus casts live audio. Persisted additively as optional scene `playlists` (ID array) in `homedj_lights.experience` and on the wire; absent (older builds) keeps the saved links, an empty array unlinks. Optional reply `lightsScenePlaylists: true` gates the OnePlus picker. No new LAN command, port, permission, dependency or WorkManager identity.
- Leaving and coming home (one card under Home detection in the OnePlus's Settings): when you leave, turn off all lights (`awayOff`) and pause music (`autoPause`, OnePlus pref); when you come home, leave lights, turn back on what leaving turned off (`homeOn`), or apply a chosen scene (`homeScene`, its own linked music is not used on arrival), and resume the paused song or start a chosen playlist (`homePlaylist`) freshly shuffled every time, unless music is already playing or a cast runs. "Left" stays Samsung's existing 5-min unseen rule and arrival its next authenticated Home check-in. Persisted additively as optional `homeScene`/`homePlaylist` in `homedj_lights.automation` (missing = none; deleting the scene clears it; whole-automation saves keep them). Wire: `lightsPresence` gains optional `homeScene`/`homePlaylist` strings (absent keeps, "" clears; older OnePlus builds cannot erase them), reply capability `lightsArrival: true` gates the OnePlus pickers. The Samsung's Lights → Automation page now points to the OnePlus card. No new command, port, permission, dependency or WorkManager identity.
- Scene editor live preview: while a scene is created or edited, the lights show the draft at once (no fade) and return to their recorded states on close, save or delete. New authenticated command `lightsPreview` (optional for older Samsungs: Unknown command); no persisted key, port, frame, permission or dependency change.
- Light variation ("alive" lights): a toggle with continuous 0–100 % amount sliders for Hue (warmth on white), Saturation and Brightness (0 = still, at least one above 0) and a Speed slider (about 20 s down to 2 s per step) for all lights, each light, each scene and each light inside a scene. Samsung's `Lights` owns the intent; `LightVariationRunner` runs only inside `RemoteServerService` (its existing wake/Wi-Fi locks), so variation needs Samsung's remote listener on. Each step is a random walk around the bulb's recorded settings, moving at most half the channel's range on from the last step (in-memory drift per bulb, so chained transitions read as one smooth drift), reached by the bulb's own transition (speed slider ±15 % jitter; 100 % is ±60° hue, ±40 saturation, ±1,600 K, ±50 % of the level, at least 15) using ColorControl with ExecuteIfOff clear and LevelControl `MoveToLevel` (0x00, no OnOff), so it never turns a light on and steps are never persisted. Native fades hold variation until they finish; turning variation off restores the record once. Unreachable bulbs back off 30 s→8 min. Persisted additively in `homedj_lights.experience.variation` (`all`, `individual[{id, v}]`), scene `variation` and scene change `v`, each a 6-bit integer (1 on, 2 high, 4 fast, 8 hue, 16 saturation, 32 brightness) that older builds read, plus, when the amounts are not the old Subtle 20 % / High 75 % and Slow 25 % / Fast 75 % values, the full value under the same key + `2` (`v2`, `variation2`, `all2`: bit 0 on, then 7 bits each for hue, saturation, brightness and speed). Older values read as those presets. New authenticated command `lightsVariation` (`variation`, optional `id`, `clear`); optional reply `lightsVariation: true` gates the OnePlus UI. Older Controllers' scene saves keep scene variation. The LAN frame limit (`RemoteChannel.MAX_FRAME`) is 131,072 bytes (was 65,536); the worst-case reply with every variation custom is about 68 KB (test keeps ≥2 KB spare), so a Controller on an older build fails to load Lights only in such a near-maximum setup. No dependency, permission, service, port, WorkManager identity or pairing change. Contract: [docs/lights-experience.md](docs/lights-experience.md). Groups: each group has an optional variation (`v` on its `configuration.groups` entry, same integer; absent = follows all lights). A light resolves its own value, then its group's, then all lights'. Setting a group's variation clears its lights' own values; the all-light value clears every group's and light's; a light joining a group follows the group. Group saves keep the stored variation. New command `lightsGroupVariation` (`groupId`, `variation` or `clear`) with optional reply `lightsGroupVariation: true` (a distinct command so a Samsung with only light/scene variation answers `Unknown command`).
- Light show ("Dance to the music"): a switch on the Lights page of both phones, a Party/Chill style and a light-timing slider (-500..+500 ms, lights earlier/later than the speakers, for Bluetooth delay). While on and the Samsung plays music, its lit lights dance; off lights stay off. Each player deck's sink is wrapped in `PulseAudioSink` (Media3 `ForwardingAudioSink`, already in media3-exoplayer 1.8.1; 1.8.1 has no `AudioProcessor.StreamMetadata`), which feeds decoded 16-bit PCM to a per-deck `BeatTracker` (100 Hz bass/broadband onset envelope, tempo 70-181 BPM by weighted autocorrelation over 6 s, phase by comb sum, grid locked only after three agreeing estimates with autocorrelation confidence >= 0.2; noise peaks near 0.13) and records the sink's audible position for timing. `LightDanceRunner` runs inside `RemoteServerService` (its wake/Wi-Fi locks), so dancing needs Samsung's remote listener on. Party: the hit light jumps to full on each beat while the previous one fades over the beat (one light: hit, then fall within the beat), each light takes the next colour on its own beat of the bar (every two beats, instantly, on a surge). Chill: lights breathe over two beats, out of step, and drift to the next colour over a bar every two bars. Brightness follows loudness relative to the song; without a steady beat both styles breathe every 1.5 s. Colours: up to four vivid hues from the current song's YouTube thumbnail (shared Coil cache, read from Samsung's own Media3 session), else fixed vivid defaults. Cues are sent early by half the smoothed bulb answer time; late cues are dropped; an unreachable light sits out 2 s→32 s. Commands are the variation ones (MoveToLevel without OnOff, colour with ExecuteIfOff clear) and are never recorded; variation pauses on dancing lights and each light returns once to its record when it stops dancing (setting off, music stopped 4 s, light off). Persisted additively as `homedj_lights.experience.dance` (`on`, `style` = `LightDanceStyle.name`, `offset`); missing reads as off. New authenticated command `lightsDance` (`dance`); optional replies `lightsDance: true` (gates the OnePlus card) and `danceStatus` (fixed text). Live casts from the OnePlus are not analysed. No dependency, permission, service, port, WorkManager identity or pairing change; real-music lock rate, timing and bulb command rate need device verification.

- Phone pairing: new server codes contain 128 random bits encoded as 26 RFC 4648 Base32 characters (A–Z, 2–7). Spaces and case are ignored for that format; SHA-256 of UTF-8 `HomeDJ pairing v1` plus a zero byte plus the decoded 16 bytes supplies the AES-256 key, with effective 128-bit secret strength. Existing 32-byte Base64URL keys remain byte-for-byte compatible and are never automatically replaced. Samsung Settings can explicitly replace a legacy code after remote control is turned off and the user confirms re-pairing. Both phones need this build for short codes; a new Controller still accepts an older Samsung's long code. The existing `remote_pairing` keys are unchanged; older apps cannot read a newly created short code, so downgrading requires re-pairing. Address, port 47188 and code are separate manual fields; other ports are rejected because the existing listener stays fixed. Samsung can reveal a QR containing JSON `homedjPairing` = 1, `host`, `port`, `code`; OnePlus scans it with the existing Play services scanner, then explicitly verifies with Pair and connect. The code and QR remain hidden until requested. QR generation uses user-approved `com.google.zxing:core:3.5.3` (Apache-2.0, GPL-3.0-compatible), retains its four-module quiet zone, and runs locally off Main. No camera permission, discovery, external QR service or LAN frame/command change. Pairing is retained through app updates/restarts and Wi-Fi loss; clearing app data or replacing the code requires re-pairing. Samsung IP changes still require updating the address or rescanning.

- Samsung remote availability: the opted-in `RemoteServerService` holds a partial CPU wake lock until destruction, including while music is paused; the existing high-performance Wi-Fi lock is used only below Android 14, which converts it to a screen-on-only lock. The server Settings card offers the user-granted battery optimization exemption (`REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS`) and Samsung Never sleeping apps guidance. A non-exported `RemoteServerRestoreReceiver` restores the existing opt-in after unlocked boot/app replacement only when the persisted role is Server; service startup checks both again before taking locks, binding Media3 or opening the listener. No new stored settings, LAN fields, dependency or player. Continuous remote availability consumes power; keep the server powered. Force-stop, explicit remote Off, restricted/OEM sleep settings and loss of Wi-Fi still prevent availability; an IP change requires updating the Controller's saved address (reserve Samsung's address at the router).
- Matter pairing startup and failure/cancellation cleanup run on `Dispatchers.IO`, alongside the existing attestation handoff and within the same serialized attempt. Native `stopDevicePairing` takes the SDK stack lock; it must never block Android Main and take the LAN command handler/UI down with it. A successful commissioning is not stopped; failure/cancellation stops once before releasing the attempt. Pairing/fabric storage and attestation acceptance policy are unchanged.

- Permanently off (per light, both phones): a switch in the light's control sheet makes HomeDJ treat the light as having no power. Nothing it does sends to that light (scenes, previews, groups, all lights, schedules, presence, house-rule steps, variation, light show) until the switch is turned back; its own controls are disabled. Stored as optional `permanentlyOff` in the `homedj_lights` bulbs JSON; older records read as off = false.
- Light groups and individual preferences: up to 16 named groups, one group per bulb, editable from either phone; bulb/group controls and weekly schedules use the existing Samsung owner and Matter command path. Latest change wins between a bulb, its group and all lights: direct bulb controls, bulb preferences, scenes and selected-bulb schedules save individual power/brightness/colour that overrides group/default choices; a later group command, group schedule or joining a group clears its members' individual power/brightness/colour so they follow the group again, and a later all-light command or all-light schedule clears every bulb's; nullable presence choices stay individual-first until explicitly cleared. Group rules follow current membership; coincident rules run all lights, groups, then selected bulbs. New optional `homedj_lights.configuration` JSON stores `groups` (`id`, `name`, `bulbs`, `preferences`) and `individual` (bulb `id` plus preference fields); existing bulb, automation and fabric records stay readable. Rule `groupId` is optional and its existing `bulbs` field retains a safe fixed-member fallback. LAN additions: `lightsGroupSave` (`group`), `lightsGroupDelete`/`lightsGroupSet` (`groupId`, change fields), `lightsPreferences` (`id`, `preferences`), snapshot `configuration`, and `lightsAutomation.groupsVersion = 1`; requests from older Controllers preserve existing group targets when saving automation. Removing a group confirms removal of its rules and keeps bulbs/individual preferences. Older binaries cannot enforce the new priority policy; use matching builds for it.
- New-light Bluetooth setup (Samsung only): with **New light** on, Samsung passes the typed 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi name/password as `CommissionParameters.setNetworkCredentials(NetworkCredentials.forWiFi(...))` and calls `pairDeviceWithCode(..., useOnlyOnNetworkDiscovery = false, ...)`, so the vendored v1.6.1.0 SDK also scans BLE (service-data filter on 0xFFF6 by discriminator, `AndroidBleManager`) and connects GATT itself. Adds manifest `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`; runtime grants are `BLUETOOTH_SCAN` + `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` on API 31+, `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` on API 29-30. Bluetooth must already be on (Android 13+ ignores the SDK's `BluetoothAdapter.enable()`). After a successful Bluetooth pairing, `Lights` saves the Wi-Fi name (never the password) as `wifi_name` in SharedPreferences `homedj_lights` to prefill the next setup. The password is kept only in `LightsViewModel` memory (cleared on role change or when the ViewModel ends) and the single pairing call: never saved, logged, put in saved state, or sent over the LAN; the OnePlus keeps the on-network share-code path. Wire: unchanged. Paired bulbs survive app restarts and in-place updates: `homedj_lights` keeps node IDs, and the SDK's `chip.platform.KeyValueStore` keeps the issuer key (`AndroidDeviceControllerKey`, reloaded by `AndroidOperationalCredentialsIssuer::Initialize`) and fabric, while the controller node ID is the SDK's fixed `kTestControllerNodeId`. Uninstalling or clearing data loses them (`allowBackup=false`).
- Add-light scan: Samsung uses the existing connectedhomeip v1.6.1.0 `discoverCommissionableNodes`/indexed results, bounded to an 8-second collection (SDK browser expires after 5 seconds, followed by resolution time); only names/discriminators reach the UI. Selected discovery results must match the share code's long or short discriminator. QR scanning adds the user-approved `com.google.android.gms:play-services-code-scanner:16.1.0` (Google ML Kit terms, alongside the existing Google Play services integration); it requires Play services and may download its scanner module on first use. Manual entry remains available, pairing remains on-network from an open multi-admin sharing window, and neither camera permission nor a new LAN listener is added. Setup codes stay only in memory and require an explicit Pair light tap. Google documents on-device image/code processing and SDK performance/utilization metrics under its ML Kit terms; these are not an Apache-2.0 license for the SDK binary. No R8/minified release support is added.

- One Kotlin Android app `:app` (`dev.homedj.app`), minSdk 29, targetSdk 35, compileSdk 36, same APK on Samsung M21 and OnePlus 12.
- Gradle 8.13, AGP 8.11.1, Kotlin 2.2.20, JDK 17; Jetpack Compose/Material 3, Media3 1.8.1, DataStore 1.1.7 and WorkManager 2.11.2.
- Server mode owns one MediaLibraryService/session and all music files. Its PlaybackPlayer uses two service-owned ExoPlayer decks for the user-approved true crossfade, with one authoritative active queue and one audio-focus request. Controller mode owns an AES-GCM paired private-LAN client (port 47188). Paired browse/track/playlist/download requests use Samsung's stored catalog; playback uses just-in-time per-song Media3 source resolution. Role persists per device; the Controller creates no music player.
- YouTube Data API v3 and Android Google AuthorizationClient `youtube.readonly` scope return owned/imported playlist metadata and track IDs. OAuth Android client must match app signing certificate. The UI offers no way to connect an account (the owner never signs in): playlists come from links read by NativeAudioSource. The OAuth code stays in place but is reachable only through a Google grant made by an earlier build, which still shows the Sync/auto-save/Disconnect card.
- Playlist metadata is persisted app-private via bounded `SavedPlaylistStore` and is account-scoped. Tokens remain in memory; disconnect clears metadata and automatic sync selection.
- Full-list browsing: both roles use lazy song rows without a 100-row cap. Samsung opens the existing account-scoped track snapshot until explicit/hourly refresh. OnePlus reuses unchanged lists using the existing catalog/download revisions, assembles initial transfers privately into one complete list (up to 5,000 rows), and persists complete visited sections in `files/remote_browse_cache.json` (version 1, atomic, 4 MB bound), scoped by a hashed pairing identity and catalog revision. Samsung packs browse replies by UTF-8 size (60,000 bytes maximum, below the unchanged encrypted-frame bound); commands, fields, offsets and old 40-row replies remain compatible. Download marks use one scan of committed audio filenames rather than per-song extension probes. Cached metadata restores without live download flags and can be browsed offline; Samsung still authorizes every command. Public thumbnail prefetch uses the existing Coil 3.3.0 disk cache, two cancellable requests and no bitmap decoding. No dependency, permission, work-name, existing storage-format or LAN field change.
- Song sorting is a per-playlist preference for playlist and Downloaded views, saved in the existing `homedj_settings` DataStore under `playlist_sort:<playlist ID>` (Downloaded pages use `playlist_sort::downloaded`). Stable string values: `REVERSE`, `TITLE_ASC`, `TITLE_DESC`, `ARTIST_ASC`, `ARTIST_DESC`, `SHORTEST`, `LONGEST`, `DOWNLOADED`; choosing `ORIGINAL` removes the key, and missing or unknown values use `ORIGINAL`. Each playlist's last choice survives visits, role changes and app restarts independently on each phone. The older device-wide `playlist_sort` key is no longer read and is left in place, so every playlist starts in playlist order after upgrade. Existing `device_role`, playlist metadata and LAN contracts are unchanged; older builds ignore the additional keys.
- **In-app** NewPipeExtractor v0.26.5 (JitPack, GPL-3.0-or-later) independently resolves playable public, non-DRM YouTube audio streams; request headers/shape mirror NewPipe's own app (DownloaderImpl, YoutubeHttpDataSource) and visionOS-client links are preferred because no PoToken provider is installed; JDK library desugaring enabled for supported Android devices. No Termux/yt-dlp/FFmpeg needed.
- `OfflineAudioLibrary` owns original audio-only M4A/WebM/Opus app-private downloaded files, WorkManager per-song background jobs, atomic temp-file renames, no per-song or total-library size quota; transfers stop when the device has less than 10 MiB free. Playlist auto-download jobs require unmetered connectivity; in-progress transfers recheck Server role. Already downloaded audio works offline via Media3.
- Whole-playlist downloads queue 250 songs at a time; `PlaylistDownloads` remembers unfinished playlists in SharedPreferences `homedj_playlist_downloads` (`playlists`) and its `PlaylistDownloadWorker` (unique `homedj_playlist_topup`) refills the queue as saves end. Playlist intent is committed before the first song is queued (including single batches), and opening Samsung's saved catalog requests an idempotent top-up; restart selection uses one scan of committed audio and skips saved, pending and failed songs. Up to four songs transfer concurrently. A failed save never waits on WorkManager backoff, so other songs continue. Within the attempt it falls back: a broken chunk (timeout, dropped connection, 5xx) resumes the kept partial immediately up to 3 times in a row, a refused link (HTTP 4xx) or one that keeps breaking is extracted once more, then up to 3 offered streams are tried best first (patterns from yt-dlp fragment retries, NewPipe DownloadMissionRecover and Metrolist link refresh). A manual save that still fails in a way time may fix (bot check, network, extractor; not unavailable songs or rejections) is remembered in SharedPreferences `homedj_download_backup` (`later`) and re-queued once by the hourly sync as a Wi-Fi save with optional work input `backup`; that backup failing again stays failed. Cancel/remove forget it. An explicit download can still try missing songs again. When YouTube refuses a song itself (typically YouTube Music "Topic" tracks: `UNPLAYABLE` "This video is not available" to NewPipe's clients), `SongSubstitute` searches regular YouTube through the same NewPipe Extractor (`SearchInfo`, `videos` filter) for the title and artist and saves the best matching upload's audio under the original video ID: every title word, length within max(10 s, 6 %), no cover/live/remix-style version unless the song is one, at most 3 tried. Bot checks and songs without a known length are not substituted. No new dependency, persisted key or LAN field.
- `AutoLibraryWorker` runs inexact unmetered hourly work in Server mode (`homedj_playlist_sync`, period updated in place from older 12-hour jobs): it refreshes up to 30 saved playlists, account and link alike, through `PlaylistRefresh`, queues songs newly added to playlists with a downloaded song as Wi-Fi-only saves (input `playlistId` without `accountKey`), and with auto-save on queues opted-in playlists, up to 150 saves per pass. It does not guarantee timely playback or download completion on Samsung background restrictions.
- The OnePlus Controller can explicitly request Android MediaProjection playback capture with RECORD_AUDIO permission, then send allowed stereo PCM over the existing encrypted paired TCP connection to a temporary Samsung AudioTrack output with the low-latency performance mode, a 50 ms startup threshold and an adaptive 50–250 ms queue that grows after dropouts and relaxes on calm Wi-Fi (API 29/30 use a reduced effective buffer); the OnePlus sends 10 ms packets with a 100 ms capture buffer and a fixed 16 KB socket send buffer. Playback capture is not allowed for apps that deny it or DRM/calls. OnePlus media volume is set to zero once per accepted cast; subsequent manual volume changes and stopping do not reset it. The cast is foreground-only, non-persistent, and its UI/service close and Wi-Fi loss terminate the stream.
- The older shared `Music/HomeDJ` MediaStore scan remains optional for legacy audio; it requires READ_MEDIA_AUDIO/READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE and never invokes Termux. No Room database or schema change.
- GPL obligations require assessment before distributing linked APKs; neither Metrolist source nor InnerTubeX is bundled. YouTube's unofficial extraction can stop working at any time and doesn't unlock restricted content.
- Weekly music schedule (Samsung only): selected ISO weekdays (1 = Monday, 7 = Sunday), start/end times to the minute in Samsung's local time (24:00 = end of day; no midnight wrap), no overlaps on shared days, at most 24 slots. Each slot plays the cached tracks (≤2,000, `MAX_PLAYLIST_SONGS`) of up to 10 library playlists, shuffled, at a STREAM_MUSIC volume. The playlist's top settings menu offers "Add to schedule" with that playlist preselected; the Schedule tab edits/deletes the same slots. `ScheduleStore` atomically writes app-private `music_schedule.json` (version 2 for weekly/minute slots, 64 KB bound): existing `start`/`end` hour fields are retained, with `days`, `startMinute`, `endMinute` added. Version 1 still loads as every day at whole hours, and all-daily whole-hour schedules keep writing version 1; version 2 is intentionally rejected by older builds so they cannot play weekly slots every day (downgrading schedule data is unsupported). Driven by the existing exact alarm and boot/update/clock-change re-arming; alarm permission is user-grantable and Samsung battery restrictions can delay execution. No LAN protocol change: the OnePlus cannot edit schedules.
- Random music breaks (Samsung plays, either phone edits): off by default. Defaults are music 15–35 min (long 45–75 min, 20%), silence 10–25 min (long 30–60 min, 20%) and 5–10 s fade-ins. Music intervals extend to the current song's natural end, fading its last 5 seconds before the full silence starts; breaks never cut a song. Stored in SharedPreferences `homedj_music_breaks` (`settings` JSON). LAN: new `setBreaks` command plus optional `breaks`/`breakSeconds`/`nextBreakSeconds` status fields; an older Samsung answers `Unknown command` and the OnePlus shows an update hint.
- Playback transitions: 8-second equal-power song overlaps with S-shaped ease-in/ease-out timing for automatic next songs and manual song/queue changes, 5-second smooth manual/scheduled play/pause fades, and the existing configured fade duration on music-break resume. A pending break suppresses the automatic overlap so its song finishes before silence. The next deck is prepared within 20 seconds of a known song end; if unavailable, Media3 advances normally. Manual replacements keep the outgoing song audible while loading for at most 15 seconds. All transition state is in memory, latest intent wins, both decks stop on casting/role change/teardown, and no new settings, dependencies, permissions or LAN fields are added.
- Loudness normalization (Samsung, always on): every song, streamed or downloaded, is leveled toward -14 LUFS (YouTube's reference) by a per-deck Media3 audio processor that measures loudness as it plays; boost is capped at +10 dB with a clip guard, cut at -12 dB. Overall level is lower than raw masters, so the Samsung volume may need raising. No new dependencies, settings, persisted keys or LAN fields.
- Playlist links work without a Google account: on Samsung (Library, Schedule) the extractor reads a public/unlisted playlist (≤2,000 songs, ≤25 pages of 100) when no YouTube token is in memory, and the result is stored as an imported playlist in the existing JSON snapshot (no new keys). LAN: new `importPlaylist` command with a `link` field; Samsung parses and validates the link itself and replies `ok`/`message`/`id`. The OnePlus waits up to 45 s for that reply (other commands keep 5 s); an older Samsung answers `Unknown command` and the OnePlus shows an update hint.
- Home detection (OnePlus decides, Samsung acts): opt-in on the OnePlus. While on, a `PresenceService` foreground service (`connectedDevice`, quiet notification showing Home/Away, plus a notification on each change) fuses three signals: the authenticated LAN `presence` check-in (every 60 s on Wi-Fi, backing off to 16 min while Samsung does not answer, immediately on joining Wi-Fi), Samsung's BLE beacon, and one 150 m Play services geofence around a home location set on the OnePlus. Home when at least 2 of 3 signals are green (Samsung answered on Wi-Fi within 3 min, its beacon seen within 3 min, inside the geofence), otherwise Away; the verdict is held only for the first 3 min after a start. GPS cannot vote without a home location and "Allow all the time". Samsung pauses once when the OnePlus has been neither seen nor heard for 5 min, and resumes only the song it paused when a check-in carries the OnePlus's Home vote and nobody pressed play since. Dependency `com.google.android.gms:play-services-location:21.4.0` (proprietary Google Play services, same family as play-services-auth). Permissions: OnePlus fine/coarse + background location ("Allow all the time"), Bluetooth scan (`neverForLocation`) and advertise, notifications; Samsung Bluetooth scan/advertise only. Persisted: OnePlus SharedPreferences `homedj_presence` (`enabled`, `auto_pause`, `home_lat`/`home_lng` as raw double bits, `geofence` = `GeofenceState.name`); Samsung keeps nothing. LAN: new command `presence` with `autoPause` and `home` (the vote); an older Samsung answers `Unknown command` and the OnePlus shows an update hint. Persisted additions, all optional so older files and builds still read: `SavedPlaylistCodec` track fields `artist`/`duration` and playlist field `art` (HTTPS `*.ytimg.com` cover only); per-download `files/offline_info/<id>.json` and `files/offline_art/<id>.jpg`; WorkManager input keys `title`/`artist`/`duration`. LAN additions, all optional: status `artist`/`position`/`duration`; `tracks`/`downloads` items `artist`/`duration`; `tracks` replies `savedCount`/`savingCount`; `catalog` items `art`; commands `seek` (`position`, `index`), `cancelSave` and `removeSaved` (`id`, `kind` = `track`|`playlist`). An older Samsung answers `Unknown command` and the OnePlus shows an update hint. YouTube sync makes one extra `videos?part=contentDetails` call per 50 songs for lengths. Artwork is loaded with Coil 3.3.0 (`coil-compose`, `coil-network-okhttp`; Apache-2.0, GPL-compatible), which brings OkHttp 4.12.0 transitively and lifts kotlinx-coroutines 1.9.0→1.10.2, okio 3.4→3.15, exifinterface 1.3.6→1.4.1, profileinstaller 1.4.0→1.4.1; Compose stays on BOM 2025.09.01 (1.9.2). Icons are hand-written vector drawables in `res/drawable` from Material Icons paths (Apache-2.0); no icon library. Song art is YouTube's public `i.ytimg.com` thumbnail derived from the video ID; the OnePlus fetches thumbnails itself and shows placeholder art for Samsung's now-playing song (the status reply carries no song ID).

- Both phones show one Activity feed of recent changes (in memory on Samsung, not persisted). LAN: optional status fields `activity` (≤8 `{id, ago, source, text}`), `library` (catalog revision) and `index` (current item), and optional command fields `play` (playPause), `on` (shuffle) and `index` (next/previous); older builds ignore them and keep toggle behavior. - Paired LAN control retains one authenticated short-lived TCP request per operation (no new transport/dependency). The Controller retries after 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and capped 30 seconds while disconnected, polls every 3 seconds when reachable (only while its UI is started), and re-polls immediately on app return, Wi-Fi availability or a failed command; one missed poll on a live link is tolerated. While remote access is on, Samsung holds a Wi-Fi lock (`WAKE_LOCK` permission) so screen-off power save does not drop paired connections. The Samsung opt-in foreground listener uses START_STICKY for Android-managed process restarts; it remains subject to OS/OEM background limits. Samsung shows its Wi-Fi IPv4 rather than a cellular/VPN address, and pairing still requires a manual private IPv4 (reserve its DHCP lease to keep it stable).

- Matter lights (Lights tab on both phones; the Samsung is the only controller): Project CHIP connectedhomeip v1.6.1.0 Android controller (Apache-2.0, GPL-3.0-compatible) vendored in `app/libs/matter` (controller jars + stripped `arm64-v8a` `libCHIPController.so`/`libc++_shared.so`), built by `.github/workflows/matter-sdk.yml` in upstream's `chip-build-android:200` container; `VERSION.txt` and `SHA256SUMS` record provenance. No Maven artifact exists and the SDK builds only on Linux/macOS. The APK is now arm64-v8a only (both phones). The Samsung pairs up to 16 Matter-over-Wi-Fi bulbs (tested model: Linkind) with a printed setup code while its first Matter pairing window is open, or a multi-admin share code from an existing Matter controller (on-network commissioning, no BLE; an AiDot connection alone does not establish a Matter pairing). It sends On/Off, LevelControl `MoveToLevelWithOnOff` and ColorControl commands on endpoint 1, to all bulbs or one, and keeps acknowledged targets and optional dated observations from an explicit Check light read. Automation on the Samsung: turn bulbs off when the OnePlus has been unseen for 5 min and turn only those back on at its next home check-in (needs the listener and OnePlus home detection on); up to 20 weekly rules (ISO days, minute, Samsung local time, on with brightness/colour or off, all or chosen bulbs) on an exact alarm (inexact when denied); rules missed by more than 15 min are skipped. Device attestation is not verified (the SDK ships only test roots; the person pairs a bulb they hold). Permissions: `ACCESS_WIFI_STATE`, `CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE` (normal, for the SDK's mDNS multicast lock); the existing exact-alarm permission. Persisted: SharedPreferences `homedj_lights` keys `bulbs`, `automation` (JSON, field names fixed), `presence_off`, `rules_ran_at`; the legacy one-bulb `node_id` is migrated into `bulbs` (read old, write new, remove old). The SDK's own app-private preferences hold the fabric root/operational keys (backup is off; clearing app data needs re-pairing). WorkManager: unique works `homedj_lights_rules` (APPEND_OR_REPLACE) and `homedj_lights_presence` (REPLACE), worker `LightsWorker`, input key `trigger` = `rules`|`home`|`away`; receiver `LightsAlarmReceiver` (not exported; alarm, boot, update, clock changes). LAN, all optional: commands `lights`, `lightsSet` (`id` optional = all, `on`, `level`, `hue`+`sat` or `kelvin`), `lightsRename` (`id`, `name`), `lightsAutomation` (`automation`); every reply carries `lights` (snapshot) and `exactAlarms`. The OnePlus waits up to 45 s for these. An older Samsung answers `Unknown command` and the OnePlus shows an update hint. Samsung remains the Matter controller. Both phones can initiate pairing/discovery and confirmed forgetting; only the setup code travels from OnePlus to its authenticated Samsung in encrypted request memory, never in snapshots/logs/storage. Fabric credentials stay on Samsung.
